About Sarah Chen

Former occupational therapist turned full-time artist. Sarah creates slip-cast ceramic covers that feel like wearable sculpture.

Sarah Chen lost her left leg in a car accident at 14. For most of her adolescence and early adulthood, she treated her prosthetic as something to be hidden — a piece of medical equipment, not an expression of who she was.

That changed in her early 30s when she started taking evening ceramics classes at the Portland Art Institute. What began as a hobby became a vocation when she started experimenting with making covers for her own prosthetic. The response from other amputees — who sought her out after seeing photos online — convinced her to build a real practice around it.

She left her OT practice in 2023 and now works full-time from her studio in Northeast Portland, making two to four covers per week. Each piece is slip-cast, hand-painted with underglaze, and finished with a final glaze firing. She tests every design on her own prosthetic before listing it.

Sarah's work has been featured in Disability Arts Online, the Portland Design Week exhibition, and The Mighty. She's been worn by athletes, artists, and everyday people in over 20 countries.
